Questions Without Answers

By A. C. Thomas — From "The Key to Truth" by E. H. Lake

Many people have a very limited view of God's plan of salvation and, when approached with questions, are unable to give an answer—or unable to give a correct answer. This can be, first and foremost, attributed to a lack of true Bible study. It can also be caused by the bias of denominational beliefs, creeds, individual interpretations, or relying on other people to answer for them.

But what does the Bible have to say about the individual Christian being able to give an answer when questioned?

"Give diligence to present yourself approved by God, a workman who doesn’t need to be ashamed, properly handling the Word of Truth."
— 2 Timothy 2:15

Believers are told to "Give diligence," or "be diligent". Why? To be "approved by God" and "not to be ashamed" when questions are asked. How and what are they told to study? By "properly handling the Word of Truth," which is the Bible.

Next, look at what the Apostle Peter says concerning questions and answers in 1 Peter 3:15:

"But sanctify the Lord God in your hearts; and always be ready to give an answer to everyone who asks you a reason concerning the hope that is in you, with humility and fear:"
— 1 Peter 3:15

Within this short verse of Scripture, there are several key principles to consider:

  1. Be READY to give an answer — be prepared.
  2. Be ready ALWAYS — do not put off a question.
  3. Be ready always to give an answer — evasion excites suspicion.
  4. Give an answer to EVERYONE who asks you.
  5. A REASON is asked for — not an assertion.
  6. The answer should be a reason of THE HOPE [not the fear] that is in you.
  7. Answer with HUMILITY toward people, and REVERENT FEAR toward God.

Below is a list of 213 questions that will help to clarify God's plan of salvation as set forth in the Holy Bible, so that when questions are asked, correct answers can be given. To those holding a limited view of God's plan of salvation, they are truly Questions Without Answers; but to those who rely on God's Word, the answers become apparent as His truth is revealed to them.

  1. Since we are required to love our enemies, can we not safely infer that God loves His enemies? (Matthew 5:44 — "But I tell you, love your enemies, bless those who curse you, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who mistreat you and persecute you,")
  2. If God loves His enemies, will He punish them more than will be for their good?
  3. Would endless punishment be for the good of any being?
  4. Since God loves His friends, if He loves His enemies as well, are not all human beings the objects of His love?
  5. If God loves only those who love Him, how is He any better than the sinner? (Luke 6:32-33 — "If you love those who love you, what credit is that to you? For even sinners love those who love them. If you do good to those who do good to you, what credit is that to you? For even sinners do the same.")
  6. Since "love doesn’t think evil," can God design the ultimate evil of a single soul? (1 Corinthians 13:5 — "doesn’t behave inappropriately, doesn’t seek its own way, is not provoked, takes no account of evil;")
  7. Since "love doesn’t harm a neighbor," can God inflict, cause, or allow to be inflicted an endless harm? (Romans 13:10 — "Love doesn’t harm a neighbor. Love therefore is the fulfillment of the law.")
  8. Since we are forbidden to be overcome by evil, can we safely suppose that God will be overcome by evil? (Romans 12:21 — "Don’t be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.")
  9. Wouldn't the infliction of endless punishment prove that God HAD been overcome by evil?
  10. If a person does wrong by returning evil for evil, wouldn't God do wrong if He were to do the same?
  11. Wouldn't endless punishment be the returning of evil for evil?
  12. Since we are commanded "overcome evil with good," can we not safely infer that God will do the same? (Romans 12:21 — "Don’t be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.")
  13. Would the infliction of endless punishment be overcoming evil with good?
  14. If God hates the sinner, does the sinner do wrong in hating Him?
  15. Is God a changeable being? (James 1:17 — "Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom can be no variation, nor shifting shadow.")
  16. If God loves His enemies now, will He not always love them?
  17. Is it just for God to be "kind toward the unthankful and evil" in their present life? (Luke 6:35 — "But love your enemies, and do good, and lend, expecting nothing back; and your reward will be great, and you will be children of the Most High; for he is kind toward the unthankful and evil.")

  19. If all people justly deserve endless punishment, won't those who are saved be saved unjustly?
  20. If God "will by no means clear the guilty," by what means can just punishment be evaded? (Exodus 34:7 — "keeping loving kindness for thousands, forgiving iniquity and disobedience and sin; and that will by no means clear the guilty, visiting the iniquity of the fathers on the children, and on the children’s children, on the third and on the fourth generation.")
  21. Since no person can measure out endless punishment to their neighbor, will endless punishment be measured out to them?   27. Does divine mercy require anything that justice refuses to grant?

  29. If the requirements of mercy oppose the demands of God's justice, can His kingdom stand? (Mark 3:24 — "If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand.")
  30. If the justice and mercy of God oppose each other in any way, do they "keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace"?

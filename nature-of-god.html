Expanded Scriptural Analysis on the Nature of God

Comprehensive Analysis of Trinitarian, Arian, and Oneness Interpretations

Across the Old and New Testaments

 

This document presents an expanded comparative study of the scripture proofs used by Trinitarianism, Arianism (Subordinationism), and Oneness (Modalism). For each viewpoint, key biblical passages are presented along with explanations of how that specific perspective interprets them, followed by cross-interpretations from the other two frameworks.

 

Overview of the Three Positions

Theological View

Core Definition

Key Hermeneutical Framework

 

Trinitarianism

One God existing eternally in three distinct, co-equal, co-eternal Persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

Distinguishes between Ontology (being/essence, where all 3 are equal) and Economy/Economy of Salvation (roles/functions, where the Son submits to the Father).

Arianism / Subordinationism

God the Father alone is the ultimate unbegotten God; the Son is a created/begotten divine entity who is subordinate to the Father in essence and authority.

Prioritizes texts emphasizing Jesus' origin, mortality, human limitations, dependence on the Father, and explicit statements where the Father is called "the only true God."

Oneness / Modalism

God is a single, undivided divine Person who manifests Himself in different modes/roles (Father in Creation, Son in Redemption, Holy Spirit in Regeneration).

Emphasizes strict numerical monotheism (the Shema) and interprets references to the Father and Son in Christ as the Spirit (deity) dwelling in human flesh (humanity).

1. Scriptures Prominently Supporting the Trinitarian View

Matthew 28:19

"Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit..."

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Focuses on the singular "name" (representing one divine authority/essence) combined with the threefold listing of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, establishing tri-unity.

●     Arian Interpretation: Views this as an ordering of authority rather than equality of nature. Being baptized under the authority of three entities does not make them equal in substance.

●     Oneness Interpretation: Highlights the singular word "name" and asserts that the singular "name" of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit is revealed in the New Testament church as Jesus (Acts 2:38).

John 1:1, 14

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God... And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us..."

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Demonstrates distinction of Persons ("with God") and identity of essence ("was God"). The pre-existent Word/Son took on human nature without ceasing to be God.

●     Arian Interpretation: Reads "the Word was God" qualitatively as "a god" or a divine being who was created by God before time and creation began.

●     Oneness Interpretation: Argues that "the Word" (Logos) was not a distinct Person in eternity, but God's mind, thought, or plan, which became embodied in human flesh as Jesus Christ.

John 10:30

"I and the Father are one."

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Asserts essential unity in nature, divine power, and deity between two distinct Persons ("I" and "the Father").

●     Arian Interpretation: Interprets this as a unity of purpose, agreement, and ministry, rather than equality of substance (analogous to believers being "one" in John 17:21).

●     Oneness Interpretation: Takes this as a direct declaration that Jesus and the Father are the exact same individual entity—the Father Himself in human form.

2 Corinthians 13:14

"The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all."

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: A classic apostolic benediction placing Jesus, God (the Father), and the Holy Spirit on equal theological footing as sources of divine grace, love, and fellowship.

●     Arian Interpretation: Notes that "God" specifically refers to the Father, while Jesus is the "Lord" and the Holy Spirit is God's power/fellowship, maintaining distinction in rank.

●     Oneness Interpretation: Views these as three distinct operations or gifts coming from the one God manifested through Christ and felt through the Spirit.

Colossians 2:9

"For in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily."

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Confirms that Christ possesses the full, unmitigated divine nature (theotes) in His incarnate person.

●     Arian Interpretation: Explains that God the Father pleased to have His divine fullness dwell in Jesus as a delegated gift (Col 1:19).

●     Oneness Interpretation: Key proof text that the total spirit of God the Father resides within the body of Jesus Christ.

Acts 5:3–4

"But Peter said, 'Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit...' You have not lied to man but to God."

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Directly equates lying to the Holy Spirit with lying to God, establishing the distinct personality and full deity of the Holy Spirit.

●     Arian Interpretation: Lying to God's Spirit is lying to God by proxy, as the Spirit is God's active force and presence, not a separate co-equal person.

●     Oneness Interpretation: The Holy Spirit is God Himself in spiritual presence, so lying to the Spirit is lying to the one God.

Genesis 1:26 & Genesis 11:7

"Then God said, 'Let us make man in our image, after our likeness...'" / "Come, let us go down and there confuse their language..."

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Point to plural pronouns ("us", "our") used by God in the Old Testament as early revelations of the interpersonal dialogue within the Trinity.

●     Arian Interpretation: God is speaking to His first creation, the pre-existent Son (or angels), as His master workman (Proverbs 8).

●     Oneness Interpretation: Uses of majestic plurals or prophetic foreknowledge where God speaks in light of the future realization of humanity and the Sonship.

2. Scriptures Prominently Supporting the Arian / Subordinationist View

John 14:28

"...for the Father is greater than I."

●     Arian Interpretation: Plainly establishes that the Father is superior in essence, position, and power to the Son, ruling out co-equality.

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Refers to Jesus' state of incarnation/humiliation (kenosis) as a human, or His functional subordination as the Son, while remaining equal in divine essence.

●     Oneness Interpretation: Means the Father (the divine spirit) is greater in power and dimension than the Son (the physical human flesh).

 

Colossians 1:15

"He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation."

●     Arian Interpretation: Indicates that the Son had a beginning in time and was the first entity created by God before all other creations.

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: "Firstborn" (prototokos) denotes preeminence, sovereignty, and rightful inheritance over creation, not being created Himself (supported by verses 16–17).

●     Oneness Interpretation: Represents Christ as the archetypal design and visible image of the invisible God within creation.

1 Corinthians 8:6

"...yet for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things and for whom we exist, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things..."

●     Arian Interpretation: Explicitly reserves the title "one God" for the Father alone, designating Jesus as a separate, intermediate agent ("one Lord").

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Paul incorporates both the Father and Jesus into the Old Testament Shema (Deut 6:4), identifying Father as Source and Son as Agent within the divine identity.

●     Oneness Interpretation: "One God, the Father" is the ultimate divine origin, while "one Lord, Jesus Christ" is the manifested mediator in history.

John 17:3

"And this is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent."

●     Arian Interpretation: Jesus addresses the Father as "the only true God," explicitly distinguishing the Father from Himself.

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Jesus distinguishes Himself functionally in His incarnate mission, contrasted against false idols, not excluding Himself from deity (cf. 1 John 5:20).

●     Oneness Interpretation: Highlights that the Father in heaven is the only true God, who sent Jesus into the world as His human incarnation.

Mark 13:32

"But concerning that day or that hour, no one knows, not even the angels in heaven, nor the Son, but only the Father."

●     Arian Interpretation: Demonstrates limited knowledge in the Son, proving He lacks omniscience and therefore cannot be co-equal God.

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Reflects Christ's human nature during His earthly ministry, wherein He voluntarily limited the independent exercise of divine attributes.

●     Oneness Interpretation: As human flesh (Son), He did not know; but as the dwelling spirit (Father), He possesses all knowledge.

 

Proverbs 8:22 (Septuagint / Early Exegesis)

"The LORD created me at the beginning of his work, the first of his acts of old."

●     Arian Interpretation: A central historical text identifying personified Divine Wisdom as the pre-existent Son who was created by Yahweh before the world.

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Views this passage as poetic personification of God's wisdom, or translates Hebrew qanah as "possessed" or "brought forth" rather than created.

●     Oneness Interpretation: Sees Wisdom as God's attribute and plan formed in His mind prior to creation.

1 Corinthians 15:28

"When all things are subjected to him, then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who put all things in subjection under him, that God may be all in all."

●     Arian Interpretation: Proves the Son's permanent, eternal subordination to God the Father even after the final restoration of all things.

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Describes the completion of the Son's mediatorial kingdom and mission, handing reign to the Father in economic Trinity order without losing ontological equality.

●     Oneness Interpretation: Marks the end of the distinct incarnate role/Sonship, as God resumes His unmediated spiritual reign as all in all.

3. Scriptures Prominently Supporting the Oneness View

Deuteronomy 6:4

"Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one."

●     Oneness Interpretation: The absolute foundation of strict numerical monotheism: God is a single, undivided personal entity.

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Affirms monotheism, arguing "one" (echad) allows for a composite unity shared by three divine Persons.

●     Arian Interpretation: Affirms strict monotheism, declaring that the Father alone is Yahweh, the absolute God.

Isaiah 9:6

"For to us a child is born, to us a son is given... and his name shall be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace."

●     Oneness Interpretation: Explicitly names the promised Son as the "Mighty God" and "Everlasting Father," proving Jesus is the Father in human flesh.

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: "Everlasting Father" is an idiomatic expression for "father/author of eternity" or protective shepherd, maintaining personal distinction between Son and Father.

●     Arian Interpretation: "Mighty God" (El Gibbor) represents delegated divine authority, distinguishable from "Almighty God" (El Shaddai).

John 14:8–9

"Philip said to him, 'Lord, show us the Father...' Jesus said to him, 'Have I been with you so long, and you still do not know me, Philip? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father.'"

●     Oneness Interpretation: Jesus directly confirms that seeing Him is seeing the Father, because the Father dwells entirely within Him.

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Jesus reveals the Father perfectly because He is the exact image of the Father's essence, though personally distinct.

●     Arian Interpretation: Jesus represents the Father so completely in word and deed that seeing the Son gives full knowledge of the Father.

Isaiah 44:6 & Isaiah 45:5

"I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god." / "I am the LORD, and there is no other, besides me there is no God..."

●     Oneness Interpretation: God explicitly states no other divine person exists alongside Him. Jesus claims the title "First and Last" in Revelation 1:17, identifying as Yahweh.

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Denies the existence of false gods while allowing the one divine essence to be shared by Father, Son, and Spirit.

●     Arian Interpretation: Proves that the Father alone is God supreme, while Christ is His created, subordinate Instrument.

Acts 2:38 & Acts 19:5

"...Repent and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins..."

●     Oneness Interpretation: Demonstrates that the Apostles understood the "name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit" (Matt 28:19) to be the single name: Jesus.

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Indicates baptism into Christ's covenant community and authority, complementing the triune baptismal formula.

●     Arian Interpretation: Shows the central role of Jesus as Savior and Lord appointed by God.

1 Timothy 3:16

"Great indeed, we confess, is the mystery of godliness: God was manifested in the flesh, vindicated by the Spirit, seen by angels, proclaimed among the nations..."

●     Oneness Interpretation: Declares plainly that God Himself (the Spirit) was manifested directly in human flesh (Jesus Christ).

●     Trinitarian Interpretation: Refers to the incarnation of the second Person of the Trinity (the Word/Son taking on flesh).

●     Arian Interpretation: Interpreted in manuscript variants as "He who was manifested in flesh," referring to Christ as God's representative.
